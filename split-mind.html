SplitMind

Multimodal AI via split learning for privacy-critical visual intelligence.

SplitMind develops a privacy-preserving multimodal AI framework for Medical Visual Question Answering, combining U-shaped split learning with a novel Question-Guided Token Pruning (QG-TP) mechanism. The architecture lets hospitals and research institutions train powerful AI models collaboratively, without sensitive medical images or diagnostic labels ever leaving the institutions that hold them.

Project Overview

A privacy-preserving architecture for collaborative medical AI

Modern vision-language models typically assume that training data from many institutions can be aggregated in a central location. In healthcare, this assumption is incompatible with privacy regulations such as HIPAA and GDPR, and with the institutional duty to protect patient information.

SplitMind addresses this gap by building privacy protection directly into the architecture. The project develops a U-shaped split learning framework for transformer-based Medical Visual Question Answering, paired with a novel Question-Guided Token Pruning (QG-TP) mechanism that removes question-irrelevant visual information before it is ever transmitted between institutions. This reduces privacy leakage at the representation level while preserving the accuracy needed for clinically meaningful tasks.

The NAIRR Pilot allocation supports large-scale transformer training, simulated privacy attacks (gradient inversion, membership inference), and robustness evaluation under realistic distributed conditions — all essential for identifying weaknesses before such systems are considered for clinical deployment.

Core contributions

  • U-shaped split learning for transformer VQA

    Vision and language encoders are partitioned between client and server, with a compressed smashed representation crossing the trust boundary.

  • Question-Guided Token Pruning (QG-TP)

    A task-aware information bottleneck that drops question-irrelevant visual tokens before transmission, tightening the privacy-utility tradeoff.

  • Adversarial privacy evaluation

    Membership inference and gradient inversion attacks quantify reconstruction fidelity (PSNR, SSIM) and attack success rates against the architecture.

  • Open release

    All code, benchmarks, and reproducibility configurations will be released to the research community.

Month 1 Report

Project initiation & research directions

Submitted · Jun 2026 +

The first reporting period focused on project planning, technical preparation, and initial implementation activities. The team finalized the research roadmap and identified three initial research directions in privacy-preserving learning for healthcare: schema-aware split learning for mental distress prediction across heterogeneous surveys, multimodal split learning for mental stress screening using survey, video, and physiological data, and broader healthcare applications including disease prediction and clinical decision support.

Significant effort was devoted to reviewing the current state of the art in split learning, federated learning, large language models, vision-language models, and privacy-preserving machine learning. Based on this review, the team refined the system architecture and identified candidate datasets and benchmark tasks for evaluating privacy-preserving collaborative learning under heterogeneous data conditions.

The graduate research assistant began implementing the baseline framework for the first research direction — the schema-aware split learning work that ultimately led to the Healthcom 2026 paper. Initial work included data collection, preprocessing pipeline development, schema analysis across survey instruments, and exploration of methods for aligning semantically related survey questions across institutions while preserving privacy.

Approximately three GPU-hours of the allocation were consumed for environment validation, dependency installation, data preprocessing, and preliminary benchmarking on NCSA Delta. No major technical issues were encountered.

  • Research directions & roadmap finalized
  • Environment validated on NCSA Delta
  • Baseline framework for schema-aware split learning initiated
  • No major technical issues; project on schedule
Month 3 Report

First accepted publication; multimodal work in development

Submitted · Aug 2026 +

The team's schema-aware split learning framework for privacy-preserving learning from heterogeneous mental health surveys has been accepted for publication at IEEE Healthcom 2026 (28th International Conference on E-health Networking, Application & Services, New York City). The framework uses LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct with LoRA to convert different survey structures into a shared natural-language representation, achieving strong performance while keeping raw survey records on the client side and substantially reducing client-side computation.

A parallel study on privacy-preserving multimodal stress screening is actively in development. This work combines facial video and physiological signals in a split-learning architecture: raw inputs remain on the client, where a lightweight visual front-end and a question-guided token pruning module reduce the information transmitted to the server. The server completes multimodal fusion and produces ordinal stress predictions with uncertainty estimates and interpretable explanations.

Approximately 43 GPU-hours of the 10,000-hour allocation have been used to date, primarily for code development, debugging, dataset preparation, and validating smaller experiments before larger sweeps. No major allocation-related issues have been encountered. GPU usage is expected to increase substantially as the project moves into full-scale evaluation.

  • Schema-aware split learning framework (LLaMA-3.2-3B + LoRA)
  • Paper accepted at IEEE Healthcom 2026 (New York City)
  • Multimodal stress screening: implementation & evaluation
  • Distributed multimodal training & privacy attack reproduction
