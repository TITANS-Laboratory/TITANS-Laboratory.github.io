About the project

The problem.

Artificial intelligence (AI) systems increasingly inform consequential decisions in education, healthcare, finance, and other fields. Today there is no efficient, standardized way to confirm that an AI model is reliable, fair, and safe before it is deployed. Checking is done manually, with fragmented and disconnected tools and no common measure of reliability. Errors or unfairness can affect large numbers of people who have no visibility into how these systems were validated.

The technology.

The team is developing an automated, domain-aware platform that evaluates the reliability of AI systems before deployment. It combines a multi-dimensional assessment pipeline with a weighted scoring framework. The platform evaluates a model across five dimensions — robustness, privacy, fairness, transparency, and accountability — and normalizes these signals into a single interpretable reliability score. The output includes a risk breakdown and clear recommendations. The goal is to make reliability evaluation faster, standardized, and accessible to people who are not specialists in each risk area.

What makes it different.

Current approaches fall into three groups: manual self-assessment frameworks, high-level governance guidelines, and single-property technical toolkits that address only one concern. The platform aims to unify these needs by translating legal, ethical, and governance requirements into measurable technical tests and combining them into one repeatable, auditable measure that can be re-run as systems are updated.

What this I-Corps project does.

This project uses experiential learning and first-hand investigation of the industry ecosystem to assess the translation potential of the technology. Through the NSF National I-Corps Teams curriculum, the team is conducting at least 100 customer discovery interviews with potential customers and partners. The interviews test hypotheses about customer segments, value propositions, and adoption barriers, and they support an evidence-based decision about the technology's path forward.

Potential impact.

The technology may reduce the time and cost of validating AI systems and support the responsible adoption of AI across sectors, including education, where the reliability and fairness of AI tools directly affect students.

The platform at a glance

The platform follows a simple pipeline. This overview is intentionally high level.

Governance requirements (for example, the EU AI Act and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework)

Intake & profiling

Collects basic information about the AI system and its deployment context, and determines which checks apply.

Automated evaluation modules

Five parallel modules, one per dimension:

  • Robustness — resilience to manipulated or perturbed inputs
  • Privacy — signs of information leakage
  • Fairness — outcome differences across groups
  • Transparency — consistency of explanations
  • Accountability — logging, traceability, and human oversight

Scoring layer

Normalizes and combines the results into dimension-level scores and one composite reliability score, with domain-adjustable weights.

Reporting

Produces an audit-ready, human-readable report with a risk breakdown and recommendations.

Optional continuous monitoring — re-runs the evaluation as models are updated or retrained (feedback from reporting back to intake).

Talk with us

Do you build, deploy, evaluate, or oversee AI systems? We would like to hear how your team checks whether AI systems are reliable, fair, and safe today, and where the process is difficult.

  • We are not selling anything. These are customer discovery conversations.
  • Conversations take about 20 to 30 minutes by video call or phone, at a time that suits you.
  • Participation is voluntary. You may decline any question or stop at any time.
  • What you share is kept confidential and reported only in summary form combined with what we learn from others.
  • Participation is open to adults (18 and older).
